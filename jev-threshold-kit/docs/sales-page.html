Why one threshold cannot work

The error has a sign, and a scalar ECE does not carry it.

Independent measurements of jev-1.13 put Choice and Score overconfident at a temperature around 3.3–3.4, and Noul underconfident at around 0.66 — on the same 900 inputs. Both read as "ECE = 0.08". They need opposite corrections.

Too hot  ·  Choice, Score

A >= 0.9 gate admits answers that are nowhere near 90% likely. You auto-act on errors at a rate the number conceals — and because Jev never emits text, there is no odd-looking output to catch your eye.

Too cold  ·  Noul

The same gate rejects answers that were fine. You pay for human review you did not need, and the escalation bill quietly eats the 400× cost saving that justified moving to Jev in the first place.

Both failures are silent, and which one you get depends on the primitive rather than on anything visible in the response. The first symptom is a number on a monthly report.

Read the right-hand block. On the two overconfident questions a raw 0.9 gate passes 100% of traffic — it gates nothing at all — at 95% and 90% precision. On the underconfident one it passes 15.9%, sending six of every seven cases to a human who did not need to see them.

Three answers you will not enjoy

It is built to tell you no.

“DO NOT AUTOMATE”

When an error costs 60× a review and the question runs at 92%, no cutoff beats sending everything to a human. The kit says so, instead of finding you a threshold that looks fine and loses money quietly.

“UNMEASURABLE”

Below a few hundred labels a perfect model and a broken one score the same ECE. The most-cited public Jev benchmark is n=60, where a flawless model scores ≈0.045 — the range it reports. The kit refuses to pretend.

Sources

The distortion figures are other people's measurements.

Not ours, and not the vendor's. Jev shipped in September 2026 and is still early access, so treat every number here — including the kit's own — as something to re-measure on your traffic. That is, after all, the entire point.

scienthoon/jev-ood-calibration — per-type signs on 900 rule-generated tickets: Choice and Score overconfident (T 3.3–3.4), Boolean underconfident (T 0.66).

SamuelSacco/jev-exploration — an 800-item difficulty gradient, ECE at 2.1–2.5× its noise floor at every tier, and the argument that an n=60 benchmark cannot measure calibration at all.

awesome-jev-by-typesafe and typesafe-jev-examples — the protocol, the three primitives, and the instruction/criteria polarity trap.

TypeSafe's own documentation: calibration “is measured across groups of predictions; it does not guarantee that an individual answer is correct,” and confidence thresholds should be tested on your own data. This kit is how you do that.
